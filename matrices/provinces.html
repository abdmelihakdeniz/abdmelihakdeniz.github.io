81 provinces: income, rent and quality of life

The x-axis uses real data: TÜİK's 2024 GDP per capita by province, and the Central Bank of Turkey's (CBRT) Q4 2025 rents for 74 provinces divided by TÜİK's 2025 household income for each province's region. Quality of life on the y-axis is a qualitative estimate based on criteria such as safety, amenities, privacy and walkability.

Ankara, Istanbul and Kocaeli have the highest GDP per capita. Rent takes the smallest share of income in Yozgat, Kırşehir and Burdur, and the largest in Van, Muğla, Diyarbakır and Istanbul.

Why do Muş and İzmir have the same rent burden?

Muş looks cheap, but relative to local income it is about as hard to rent in as İzmir. Rent in Muş is about 40% of İzmir's, and household income in its region is about 43% of İzmir's.

Rent is for a 100 m² home (CBRT, Q4 2025). Income is TÜİK's 2025 average equivalised household disposable income per person, divided by 12. Muş uses the Van–Muş–Bitlis–Hakkari region figure.

Muğla is different: its region's income is a little below İzmir's, but tourism and holiday-home demand push its rent about 29% higher, so rent takes more than a full month's per-person income.

How to read it

  • Income view: Further right means a higher GDP per capita; higher up means better quality of life. Dashed lines mark the median of the 81 provinces.
  • Rent view: The monthly rent for a 100 m² home as a share of one person's monthly income in that province's region. The dashed line is the Turkey average (81%). Further left means rent is easier to afford; the top-left quadrant is the best value.

Highlights

  • Most affordable: Yozgat (43%), Kırşehir and Burdur (45%), Kayseri and Aksaray (48%). Rents in Central Anatolia are low relative to regional income.
  • Least affordable: Van (111%), Muğla (107%), Diyarbakır (102%) and Istanbul (101%). In Van and Diyarbakır incomes are very low; in Muğla and Istanbul rents are very high.
  • Big cities: Ankara is the most affordable of the three (56%), İzmir sits near the average (75%), and Istanbul takes a full month's income (101%).
  • Kocaeli: At 81% it is average. GDP per capita made Kocaeli look rich, but much of that output is industrial and doesn't reach household pockets.

Method

  • Rent: CBRT unit rents for valued homes, Q4 2025, scaled to 100 m².
  • Income: TÜİK, Income and Living Conditions Survey 2025 (reference year 2024), average annual equivalised household disposable income per person, by NUTS-2 region (26 regions). Each province gets its region's value.
  • Exact vs. range: TÜİK publishes the exact figure for only four regions: Ankara (₺449,618), Istanbul (₺434,929), İzmir (₺405,896) and Van–Muş–Bitlis–Hakkari (₺172,552). For the other 22 regions only a five-band map is published, so the band midpoint is used and the tooltip shows the resulting range (roughly ±8%).

Limits

  • The CBRT does not publish rent data for 7 provinces (Ardahan, Bayburt, Bingöl, Gümüşhane, Hakkari, Şırnak, Tunceli); they appear only in the income view.
  • Provinces in the same region share one income figure, so differences between them come from rent alone.
  • A 100 m² home is a family-sized flat, while income is per person; shares above 100% do not mean nobody can afford rent, only that one person's income would not cover it.
  • Quality of life on the y-axis is an estimate, not measured data.

Sources
