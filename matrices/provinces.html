81 provinces: income, rent and quality of life

The x-axis uses real data: TÜİK's 2024 GDP per capita by province, and the Central Bank of Turkey's (CBRT) Q4 2025 average rents for 74 provinces. Quality of life on the y-axis is a qualitative estimate based on criteria such as safety, amenities, privacy and walkability.

Ankara, Istanbul and Kocaeli have the highest GDP per capita. Rent is highest in Istanbul, Muğla and İzmir, and lowest in Mardin, Muş and Ağrı.

How to read it

  • Income view: Further right means a higher GDP per capita; higher up means better quality of life. Dashed lines mark the median of the 81 provinces.
  • Rent view: Average monthly rent for a 100 m² home. The dashed line is the Turkey average (₺22,475). Further left means cheaper; the top-left quadrant is the best value (cheap and high quality).

Highlights

  • Most expensive: Istanbul (₺36,786), Muğla (₺32,582), İzmir (₺25,218), Çanakkale (₺23,550) and Antalya (₺23,090).
  • Cheapest: Mardin (₺9,400), Muş and Ağrı (₺10,000), Adıyaman (₺10,811) and Yozgat (₺11,000).
  • Best value: Eskişehir, Ankara and Kayseri pair below-average rent with above-median quality of life.

Method

  • Rent: CBRT unit rents for valued homes, Q4 2025, scaled to 100 m².

Limits

  • The CBRT does not publish rent data for 7 provinces (Ardahan, Bayburt, Bingöl, Gümüşhane, Hakkari, Şırnak, Tunceli); they appear only in the income view.
  • Quality of life on the y-axis is an estimate, not measured data.

Sources

  • Habertürk: CBRT unit rents by province, Q4 2025, 74 provinces (in Turkish)
  • Habertürk: TÜİK 2024 GDP per capita by province, 81 provinces (in Turkish)
