81 provinces: income, rent and quality of life

The x-axis uses real data: TÜİK's 2024 GDP per capita by province and the Central Bank of Turkey's (CBRT) Q4 2025 rent values for 74 provinces. Quality of life on the y-axis is a qualitative estimate based on criteria such as safety, amenities, privacy and walkability.

Ankara, Istanbul and Kocaeli have the highest income; Kocaeli and Ankara have the lightest rent burden, while Van, Şanlıurfa, Diyarbakır and Muğla have the heaviest.

How to read it

  • Income view: Further right means richer; higher up means better quality of life. Dashed lines mark the median of the 81 provinces.
  • Rent burden: (provincial rent ÷ Turkey average) ÷ (provincial income ÷ Turkey average). 1.0 is the national average; further right means rent is heavier relative to local income. Best value is the top-left quadrant.

Highlights

  • Lightest burden: Kocaeli and Ankara (~0.6), followed by Karaman, Kayseri and Aksaray. Income is high relative to rent.
  • Heaviest burden: Van (1.76), Şanlıurfa and Diyarbakır (1.54), then Muğla (1.39) and Bartın (1.37). In the southeast rent isn't high in absolute terms, but GDP per capita is very low; Muğla's rent is pushed up by tourism.
  • Istanbul and İzmir: Expensive, but incomes are also high, so both sit close to the national average (1.03 and 1.01).
  • Why Muş and İzmir look the same (~1.0): Rent in Muş is about 40% of İzmir's, and income is about 40% of İzmir's too. TÜİK's household income survey confirms this: rent takes roughly 70% of monthly per-person income in the Van–Muş–Bitlis–Hakkari region and roughly 75% in İzmir.

How reliable is GDP per capita?

Below, the rent burden is recomputed with TÜİK's 2025 equivalised household disposable income for the four regions the press reports (the full 26-region table is only on TÜİK's interactive portal).

The order mostly holds, but GDP per capita understates Istanbul's burden and overstates the southeast's, where large households pull per-person GDP down. Treat differences under ~0.15 as noise.

Limits

  • The CBRT does not publish rent data for 7 provinces (Ardahan, Bayburt, Bingöl, Gümüşhane, Hakkari, Şırnak, Tunceli); they appear only in the income view.
  • GDP per capita is a proxy for household income. In industrial provinces (Kocaeli, Bilecik) and in Tunceli it overstates take-home pay.
  • Income is from 2024 and rent from Q4 2025. Both are indexed to the national average, so the time gap largely cancels out.
  • Quality of life on the y-axis is an estimate, not measured data.
